# Laptop buying guide

## RAM
8GB RAM is enough for browsing, documents and light coding. 16GB RAM is the sweet spot for programming: IDEs, Docker, browsers with many tabs and a local database run comfortably. 32GB is only needed for virtual machines, large data work or heavy video editing. For college use, 8GB is workable but 16GB gives better longevity.

## Storage
A 512GB SSD is the practical minimum for developers because toolchains, node_modules and containers grow quickly. SSDs are far faster than hard drives for boot and project builds. NVMe SSDs are faster than SATA SSDs.

## Processors
AMD Ryzen 5 and Intel Core i5 are mainstream and handle programming well. Ryzen 7 and Core i7 add more cores for faster compiles. Ryzen 5 is a good choice for programming: its multi-core performance is strong for the price. U-series chips favour battery life; H-series chips favour performance but run hotter and drain the battery faster. Apple M-series chips combine high performance with very long battery life.

## Displays
IPS panels have good viewing angles and accurate colours at a low price. OLED and AMOLED panels have perfect blacks, higher contrast and richer colour, but cost more and can show burn-in over many years. For coding, a sharp FHD IPS panel with good brightness is enough; OLED is a bonus for media. Refresh rates of 120Hz or 144Hz make scrolling smoother and matter mostly for gaming.

## Battery
Real-world battery life is usually 60-70% of the manufacturer claim. Gaming laptops with dedicated GPUs usually last 4-6 hours, while thin-and-light laptops with U-series chips reach 8-12 hours.
